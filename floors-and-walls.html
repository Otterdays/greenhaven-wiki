BUILDING BLOCKS

Floors, Walls and Crafted Grounds

Twenty-two tiles build a house, a courtyard or a path. Every one is a "family tile plus a four-neighbour join" - paint them and the corners pick themselves.

The cobblestone build family

The four cobblestone tiles are the classic building pieces. Three of them carry the wall shape (wall, doorway, archway) and share one wall-link rule.

  • Cobblestone wall (37) - blocks movement.
  • Cobblestone doorway (38) - the wall with a walk-through gap.
  • Cobblestone archway (39) - the wall with an open top, walkable.
  • Cobblestone paving (43) - a floor tile, walks like grass.

The wall-link rule looks at the four neighbours of a wall cell and picks the right piece: run, corner, T, cross, or end cap. Paint a wall one cell at a time and every corner appears without you placing it. The doorway and the archway share the same link, so a doorway can sit in the middle of a wall and the wall will stitch on both sides.

Interior floors

Three walkable floors fit inside a building. They share one floor-join so an interior room does not paint a visible seam between plank and paving.

  • Wood plank floor (40) - a wide plank deck.
  • Wood board floor (41) - a narrower interior board.
  • Interior floor (42) - the plain inside tile a cottage defaults to.

Crafted grounds

Twelve ground tiles split into two families of six. Any one in a family joins every other one in the same family; the two families never cross.

A wood ground and a stone ground meet at a hard edge - the painter drops a curb so the two do not look half-painted into each other. Within a family, the neighbours blend.

Fences and hedges

The village fence family joins each other the way the walls do. Two looks share one link so a builder can mix picket and stone freely:

  • Picket fence (80)
  • Low stone fence (81)
  • Open gate (82) - walkable, joins the fence on both sides like a doorway.
  • Closed gate (83) - same frame, bars down, blocks movement. Needs fence on two opposite sides.

The hedge (84) is a separate link family. A hedge never merges into a fence line, so a hedge row can sit behind a fence without one eating the other.

Adding a new floor or wall later

A new wall is one more tile in the wall-link family - the corner picker already knows how to stitch it. A new ground family (a fourth family of six, say) is one more is_…_ground predicate and one more set of curb rules where it meets every existing family. The join contract scales; the tile grid does not have to change.
