Live Atlas

A running world can be read. The atlas is a browser window that shows the world's map the way the world sees it right now - tiles, tree and rock depletion, and a small marker for every wanderer walking around.

What you see

  • The tile grid. One cell per map tile, painted in the atlas's one-colour-per-family palette. A meadow is green, a dock is warm wood, a fishing spot is blue, a fence is a stone grey. Every family has its own colour so a whole map reads at a glance.
  • Height shading. A cell's height tints the flat family colour, so a stepped hillside shows its steps.
  • Depletion. A resource node a player has just harvested goes dark until its respawn timer runs out. The atlas is the fastest way to see which nodes are on cooldown across the whole world.
  • Player dots. One marker per online character, in their walking position. A crowded hamlet is obvious; a lonely hermit at the far dock is obvious too.

Read only

The atlas has no editing controls. It asks the running world for its current map and player list, and paints what it hears. Nothing it shows is a copy that can drift - it is the same snapshot the client renders from. If the world moves a tree, the atlas moves it on the next refresh.

How it is different from the map painter

  • Map painter is where a map is made. Full edit tools, a tile palette, resource-node overrides, character placement. It works on files.
  • Live atlas is where a running world is watched. No tools, no edit, one view. It works on the live snapshot from a world that is up and joined.
  • Both share the same tile-family sheet, so a tile's colour is the same in both.

Where it fits

The atlas lives beside the map painter in the same developer tool. Its header reaches back to the World tools hub, the painter and the launch panel. Anyone working on a map flips between the two: paint a change, restart the world, look at the atlas to see the change is live.
