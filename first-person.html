VIEW

First Person

Press F5 to stand inside Greenhaven instead of looking down at it. The same world, the same tiles, the same characters - just from eye height, with a mouse that turns your head.

How to open it

  • Keyboard: press F5. F5 is on purpose - a rebindable slot only ever holds an A-Z letter, so nothing else can steal the view toggle.
  • Gamepad: the East button (the right-side face button). If you rebound Confirm, Back or Pause onto East, that rebound wins and F5 keyboard is the only toggle.

Press it again to return to the top-down view. The world keeps walking, mining, chopping and fighting through the switch.

Looking around

  • Mouse: move it left and right to turn (yaw), up and down to look over a wall or into a dip (pitch).
  • Right stick: pushes yaw and pitch at a rate you can hold. Full tilt turns faster than a slow drag.
  • Smooth eye: turning is eased, so the world does not snap between frames. A flick of the mouse settles over a fraction of a second.

True proportions

The horizontal field of view follows the window aspect, so a wide window shows more world sideways without stretching it. Walls and doorways look like themselves at any window size - the old fixed projection squashed everything wide and made a brick path read as a runway.

The sky is alive

Above the horizon the game paints sky every frame: sun, drifting cloud banks, distant pixel ridges, and a scatter of star motes. The sun and the ridges stay pinned to a world position, so the sky turns with you - you can pick a mountain and walk toward it. A soft vignette sits at the edges so the frame reads like a view, not a viewport.

Reduced motion

If you have motion reduction on, the clouds and the star motes hold still. The world keeps walking and swinging - only the sky goes quiet.

What you still see

The first-person view draws the same tiles as the top-down view: cobblestone walls read as walls, trees stand as silhouettes (not blocks), water stays at the horizon where it should. Every other player, mob, pet and dropped coin pile is a billboard in the same world. Look up and you see a mountain; look down and you see your own boots and any worn gear you carry.

Not a save field

The view choice is session-only. Log out and log back in and the world starts in top-down. This is on purpose: the view is a presentation choice, and the server does not need to know how you like to see it.

Where the numbers come from

All twelve tile families - the same tile ids you can read about on Terrain and Tiles - decide how a wall, a fence or a tree is drawn at eye height. The shared rules never fork; only the camera does.
