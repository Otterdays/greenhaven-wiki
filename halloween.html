Halloween

The Halloween round brings the spooky side of Greenhaven: twenty-six props, seven creatures and a boss, four costumes, two treats, two familiars, a diggable pumpkin vine ground, trick-or-treat gifts, mob loot drops, and a whole haunted town to place them in. Like the festive set, none of it is seasonal logic - a map simply places the props, a character simply wears the costumes, and the world simply spawns the mobs.

Halloween props

Twenty-six placeable tiles, all blocked single-cell scenery on the same market sheet as every other prop. Two of them - the cemetery gate and the giant cobweb - fill a 2 x 2 footprint. The lit pieces flicker and cast a soft halo (see Glow and mood).

Pumpkin vines

A new walkable ground tile (188) you can dig for pumpkins. Lay down pumpkin vine tiles in a patch, walk up to one, and dig: you get 1-2 pumpkins each time with a small chance of getting nothing. Pumpkins feed the witch cauldron recipes for costumes and candy corn. The vine is a natural ground like sand or clay, so pieces can stand on it and it blends into neighbouring tiles.

Halloween townies

Two peaceful souls wander Hallowmere beside the mobs. Both are townies: they wander, they talk when clicked, nothing can attack them, and neither is placed in any world yet - a builder drops them from the map painter's Halloween Townies character group and gives them a name, a route and lines in the NPC panel.

  • Boggs the Lamplighter - a small, hunched, grey-haired night watchman in a black tophat with an amber band, round spectacles and a long dark coat with brass buttons, a lit lantern in his free hand. He shuffles rather than struts, and he is drawn at three times the pixel density of the other townsfolk so his spectacles catch the light: a glint on each lens and a small bridge between them. His story: Hallowmere's ghost lanterns burn on remembered flame and go out every dawn, and someone must walk the lanes at dusk and light them again - forty-one Octobers, never a night missed. While the lane lamps burn, whatever walks out of the churchyard at night stays on the churchyard side of the fence; the lamps are the fence. A pale moth named Wick perches on his shoulder, his ring of brass keys hangs at his waist, and soot darkens one cuff. Since last October one lamp in his rounds will not light, and he has not slept since: when he closes his eyes he sees every lamp lit but one, and he cannot remember which.
  • Beadle Thwick the Third - the parish official: round, short-legged and strutting in a tall black cone hat with a red band, a parchment ruff at the chin, a gold chain of office with a wax seal, and the town ledger under one arm. He keeps the roll of every soul in the parish, living or otherwise, and reads the bylaws aloud whether the weather suits him or not. He calls himself the Third because there were two better ones before him. There were not.

Costumes

Four wearable pieces, all brewed at the witch cauldron (tile 165) - a placeable crafting station like the anvil and the furnace. Stand within reach of one you have placed and its recipes are offered. The three head pieces are light armour; the lantern is carried in the weapon hand and is cosmetic - it fights as Unarmed and gives no light on its own.

  • Witch hat - Head, armour 1. A tall cone with a wide brim.
  • Cat-ear hood - Head, armour 1. A black hood with two ears.
  • Pumpkin helm - Head, armour 2. A glowing carved pumpkin you wear.
  • Pumpkin lantern - Weapon hand, cosmetic. Carried, not swung.

Make more like them in the Gear Forge. How wearing works is on Equipment.

Treats

  • Pumpkin - the raw ingredient. Trade it or cook it.
  • Candy corn - a sweet you can eat ("Sweet, waxy and a little chalky. Somewhere a dentist smiles."). Six come from one pumpkin at the cauldron.

Food items show an Eat button in the Inventory; pressing it uses one and prints the item's eating message. A bottled potion shows a Drink button instead - it puts health or sprint breath straight back (see Items). Both treats feed the costume recipes: pumpkins and candy corn are the inputs for every piece above. Full item list on Items.

Familiars

Two spooky pets join the Pets roster, summoned from the Pets tab:

  • Black Cat familiar - a witch's cat with an orange bow. Pounces on your foes and keeps a small treat pouch.
  • Vampire Bat familiar - a tiny bat that flutters at your shoulder, snatches coins and carries two things in its claws.

Both familiars now have an in-world source: kill the matching mob and it has a low chance to drop the pet (Black Cat mob 8%, Vampire Bat mob 6%). An admin can still hand one out with /give or the panel.

Glow and mood

The lit props - lanterns, candles, the cauldron, the scarecrow and the altar, and the haunted windows - flicker and cast soft light halos. Two new knobs in Settings > Graphics > Look turn the atmosphere up or down:

  • Glow - how bright the flicker and halos read.
  • Mood - a full-screen look: Off, Vignette (default), Dusk or Haunted. Hiding the HUD (F10) keeps the mood up, so a screenshot still has the look.

See Settings for every graphics control.

Spooky weather

Over the six Halloween grounds (the graveyard dirt through foggy stone, tiles 156-160, plus the pumpkin vine at 188) the world gets its own atmosphere: drifting fog banks, rising green wisps, and a rare lightning flash followed a beat later by thunder. It only happens while you stand near that ground, so the rest of the map stays clear. Like every ambient effect it is per-player and unsynced, and Reduced motion turns the flash off. A builder tunes fog, wisps and storm in the map painter's Settings > Halloween weather, in plain words.

Trick-or-treat

NPCs can now hand you a gift through a dialogue option. A builder sets a Gift item and quantity on any answer in the map painter's dialogue editor. When a player clicks that option, the world gives them the item. Check Once to limit it to one claim per character for the lifetime of that character - perfect for an event where every villager gives you candy the first time you visit. The claim is saved with the character, so logging out and back in does not reset it.

Mob loot

When you kill a monster, the world now rolls its loot table (authored in the mob stats) and gives you whatever drops. Each entry has an item, a min/max quantity, and a drop chance. You see a "You get: ..." message listing what landed. The Pumpkin King and other Halloween mobs can drop pumpkins, candy corn, or any other registered item - all data, no code change needed.

Hallowmere and the stamps

The Halloween town region ships with six stamps so a builder can lay down a spooky street in one click: the graveyard row, the pumpkin patch, the spooky festival green, the town gatehouse, the lit crossroads and the Halloween market row. Each shows a plain-language description in the stamp picker. How stamps work is on The Map Painter.

How this is built

Nothing here needs seasonal code. A prop is one tile id on the market sheet. A costume is one EQUIPMENT row plus its worn art. A creature is one registry row, one stat row and one sprite sheet. A builder places them; the world reads the same numbers any other content uses. See Seasonal Content for the same pattern on the festive side.
