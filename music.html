Music and Audio

Greenhaven's soundtrack is composed in code, rendered to WAV, and shipped beside the game. Six songs play across the menu, the world, and the music-maker workshop.

Where the River BendsThe world's main theme - the shipping map.

The music player

The in-game music player is a small window that lists every bundled song. It supports:

  • Real seek. Drag the playhead anywhere in the track and the song jumps to that second, not to a bar boundary.
  • Volume rail. One slider for the player, independent of the master volume in Settings.
  • Live spectrum. A rolling bar display of what you are hearing right now, drawn from the audio sink every frame.
  • Keyboard layer. Space plays or pauses, arrow keys seek a few seconds, and Page Up / Page Down change tracks. Nothing the player holds can steal a movement key while you are in the world.

How a song is made

Every track is composed as a small Python script that reads a table of notes, tempo, and voice assignments, and renders a WAV with one of the workshop's synthesis engines. No external audio files or MIDI are involved; the source of a song is the script that generated it.

  • engine.py is the shared synthesis: an oscillator, a drum kit, and an ADSR envelope.
  • samples.py defines reusable voices and patterns.
  • songs.py holds the note tables and structure for each track.
  • workshop.py is the driver: python workshop.py <song> renders one named song to WAV.
  • make_*.py is one small script per song so re-rendering a single track does not touch the others.

Where the audio files live

Every shipped WAV sits beside the game's other bundled art in assets/audio/. The installer packs them with the client, so the songs are available offline - a world server never plays audio to a client.

Sound effects

The rest of the game's sound is procedural. Menu blips, cast tones, footfalls, anvil rings, axe impacts and level-up chimes are all synthesised at runtime by the client, and each is a small function with a fixed envelope. Their volume lives on a separate SFX rail in Settings > Audio so a player can duck music and keep impacts loud.

Related

The audio volume rails, mute toggles and the mute-when-unfocused flag are all documented on Settings. Music never interrupts a combat cue - the SFX rail is independent so a lullaby cannot cover a hit.
