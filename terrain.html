SYSTEM

Terrain and Tiles

Every tile in Greenhaven has an id from 0 to MAX_TILE (currently 119). Ids never renumber - a new tile is appended. Twelve families decide what each one looks like, whether you can walk on it, and which neighbours it joins.

The map itself

A map is a grid of one byte per cell, plus a matching height map, plus a handful of sidecars: nodes, signs, chests and campfires. A fresh map is bounded by MAX_DIM on each side so a hostile client cannot send a huge one.

Height

Every cell has a height from 0 to MAX_HEIGHT (3). Walking from a lower tile to a higher tile that is not a ramp is refused; ramps (tile 24) let a foot cross a one-step edge. The height also lifts the sprite, so a taller cliff face reads as a wall from the ground.

Join families

Four families carry a "link" rule: the client looks at the four neighbours of a tile and picks a shape (run, corner, T, cross, end cap) that stitches the tile into a line. This is what makes a curved cobblestone wall or a straight picket fence paint itself without an author drawing every corner. The four link families are:

  • Wall link - cobblestone wall, doorway and archway.
  • Fence link - picket fence, stone fence, open gate, closed gate. The two looks share one link, so a line may mix freely.
  • Hedge link - the hedge row on its own. It never merges with a fence.
  • Water and wood joins - shore edges and deck edges pick their art from the same 4-neighbour mask.

What is walkable

Everything in a "walkable" row above lets a foot through. Blocked tiles - trees, rocks, all props, walls, closed gates, hedges, fences - stop you at the tile edge with the shared RADIUS (0.24 tiles). Water is blocked to a wanderer, but every water-bird character (duck, swan, duckling, swanling, fowl) walks on it - the client and the world share the water family for exactly that test.

The full range today

Tile 0 through tile 119, and the highest placeable is the gingerbread house (119). Tiles 120 and up are reserved - a new prop or a new family appends there, never renumbers an old one.

Where a tile's meaning is set

All twelve families are one shared file the world, the client, the map painter and the atlas read from the same place. That is why the wiki's tile IDs match what the painter shows and what the world enforces.
