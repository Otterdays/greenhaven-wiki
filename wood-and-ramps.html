Wood, Ramps and Height

Three bridge tiles carry a wanderer across water. The ramp tile carries them up a one-step hill. Every tile has a height from 0 to 3, and the height map is what makes a dock meet a shore without a ladder.

The wood family

Three walkable tiles make up the wood family, and they share one deck join so a shore can flow into a pier without a visible seam.

TileIDFeel
Boardwalk7A planked path over water. Wide, and the join you use for a long deck.
Dock8A pier-side walk with visible posts. Reads as the edge of a boardwalk.
Corduroy9Log-track over a bog. A rough road, laid on wet ground.

The painter looks at the four neighbours of each wood tile and picks the deck art that stitches them. A boardwalk next to a boardwalk becomes one continuous plank. A boardwalk beside a dock picks an edge piece. The client reads the same rule at runtime, so a builder never has to place the corner themselves.

The ramp

Ramp (tile 24) is the only tile that lets a wanderer cross a one-step edge without a jump. It rises from the base height to base + 1 in a fixed direction.

  • Four directions east, south, west, north. The painter's R key rotates the rise in place.
  • A ramp is walkable. A cell one step taller without a ramp is a wall - you stop at the edge.
  • The client picks the correct visual from the ramp's stored direction, so a line of four ramps facing north reads as a four-step staircase going up.

Height

Every cell in a map has a height from 0 to MAX_HEIGHT, which is 3. The height is a second grid beside the tile grid. A cell is not taller than 3 - that is the whole ceiling of the world. Three is enough for a hill, a wall on a keep, or a stepped pyramid. Four would need a fifth tier of ramp art, and three is what the world ships.

The Raise tool in the map painter (key E) lets a builder lift or drop a cell one step at a time. Press E again to leave raise mode.

Walking on height

The client and the world share one rule: a wanderer can step into a neighbour cell only if that cell's height is the same or exactly one step higher with a ramp facing the walk direction. Every other difference blocks. A wanderer cannot jump - there is no jump key today.

Height and the sprite

A taller cell lifts the sprite drawn on it. A wanderer at the top of a hill paints slightly above their feet-line on a lower tile, and their shadow stays on the ground. This is what makes a hill read as a hill from the top-down view - not just a colour change.

The water family

The three shore tiles water (2), fishing spot (6) and the reed family share one shore join. A boardwalk over the top of the shore paints the deck plank running into the water; a dock at the edge paints a post going down. The rule is the same "look at the four neighbours" trick as the wood family.
