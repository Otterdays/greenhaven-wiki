Guilds and Friends

A guild is a small, named group with a short crest in front of every member's name. A friend list is a per-account set you can whisper from - documented on its own page, Friends. Both live on the world server, and both have tight bounds.

Guild names

  • Length: 3 to 24 characters.
  • Alphabet: letters, digits, spaces, underscores and hyphens only. Anything else refuses the name.
  • A name is a phrase - "The Mossy Lantern" fits; the length is deliberately longer than a character name and short enough to fit one panel row.

Guild tags

The tag is the short crest printed before a member's name: [LNTN] Ranger. Tags are 2 to 4 characters and appear on every chat line and every name tag. A guildless player has no bracket.

Caps

WhatCap
Members per guild24
Guilds per world32
Friends per account100

The friend cap is generous enough to fit one scrolling panel and small enough that the world never has to page the list.

The Guild Board

Press G in a world to open the Guild Board - a modal that lists every guild on the world with its name, tag, member count, and how many are online right now. The same Esc that closes the chat or the map closes the board, and one Esc closes one thing: chat first, then map, then board, then the pause menu. While the board is open the world holds your character still, so a stray keystroke cannot walk you off a cliff mid-browse.

Making a guild

  1. Open the board and press Found.
  2. Type a name (3 to 24 letters, digits, spaces, underscores or hyphens - "The Mossy Lantern" fits) and a tag (2 to 4 characters - "LANT").
  3. The world validates both fields and refuses a duplicate before it writes anything. A refused name is a plain line on the panel, never a chat message.
  4. You are the guild's owner. The board lists you as a member and prints the tag in front of your name in chat and on every name tag.

Joining and leaving

Pick a guild on the board and press Join. The world checks the member cap (24) and the per-world guild cap (32), refuses if either is full, and adds you. Every chat line you send from the second you join prints with the new tag, and a viewer that had you tagged with an old guild sees the change on the next repaint.

Leave puts you back in the guildless state, and the tag comes off your name. A world rewrites the roster for every viewer the same tick. If you are the last member out, the guild disbands - the row disappears and its tag is freed for someone else to reuse.

Requests are not built yet

There is no invitation, request, or accept step for a guild today - you either found one (you are the owner) or you join one you see on the board. The same is true of friends: adding a friend is one-way. The board is designed so a request flow can be added later without renaming anything.

Throttle

Creating, joining and leaving are throttled to one per connection per second, because each accepted change writes the database. A held click does not queue a hundred changes; the world answers the second, third, and fourth within the same second with a plain refusal.

How a tag is chosen at chat time

The world owns the tag. When a chat or whisper line leaves the server, it looks up the sender's guild and prefixes the tag - never the client's word. A player who has just joined a guild starts showing the new tag on their next line, and a player who has just been welcomed into the world (before their guild id arrives) never inherits a tag by accident.

Whispers

A whisper is one line of chat sent only to the sender and one named target. Same 160 character cap as public chat, same control-character strip, same guild tag on both sides. See Chat and Commands for the syntax, and Friends for opening a whisper from the panel's row menu.

Friends

Every account keeps a friend list of up to 100 characters, keyed by character id (not by display name, because two accounts can share one name). The world answers a client's friend request with the current list, so opening it is cheap and stays honest after a name change. Adding and removing a friend is throttled per account so a hostile client cannot spam the database. The panel, live presence, and the /friend and /unfriend commands have their own page: Friends.

Saved or not

Guild membership, the guild list, and every friend edge are saved with the world database. They survive a restart and follow the character, not the running client.
