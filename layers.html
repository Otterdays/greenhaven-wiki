Worn Gear Layers

A wanderer can wear a helm, a platebody, platelegs, boots, gloves, a cape and a weapon at the same time. All of them paint over the same body in one fixed order, from the back-most layer to the front-most. That order is the layers list.

Why a fixed order matters

A piece of gear is one sprite drawn over the body's current frame. If a cape drew on top of a helm, the helm's crown would vanish. If a platebody drew under the legs, a belt would disappear behind a plate skirt. The order decides who wins a pixel - and it needs to be one list, not a per-item rule, so two wearables never disagree at runtime.

The twenty layers

The list is EquipSlot::LAYERS in the shared crate. The client paints pieces in that order, first to last, so the last one covers what came before:

  1. Back - a cape. Sits behind the whole body.
  2. Legs - platelegs, trousers.
  3. Feet - boots, sandals.
  4. Chest - platebody, jumper.
  5. Waist - a belt.
  6. Wrists - bracers.
  7. Hands - gloves.
  8. Shoulders - pauldrons.
  9. Neck - a necklace.
  10. Face - spectacles, a mask.
  11. Head - a helm, a hat.
  12. Earring
  13. Ring L
  14. Ring R
  15. Trinket
  16. Pouch
  17. Ammo
  18. Off-hand - a shield.
  19. Tool (Skilling) - a held hammer or axe.
  20. Weapon - the last and top-most layer, so a sword always reads in the hand.

How a piece rides the body

Every worn overlay is frame-aligned to the character sheet it dresses. When the body changes frame - walking, running, swinging, blinking, crouching - the piece reads the same frame index on its own sheet and paints there. That is why a helm follows a head turn without any per-pose code, and why a platebody stays over the torso mid-stride.

Held swings paint the same way

The weapon and tool slots paint on top of every armour piece. When a wanderer swings, the swing clip and the held overlay share the same frame index, so the sword stays in the hand all the way through a slash and the axe head points where it should on a hack. There is no separate animation for a weapon - the piece rides the arm.

What the cape slot is not

The Back slot is reserved for the cape system, not this layer list's EQUIPMENT rows. A cape rides its own field on Skills::cape and every snapshot, so it stays visible even on a build with no EQUIPMENT row for the cape. The layer order still paints it behind everything - that is the whole point.

Adding a new slot later

The list can grow. A new layer appends where it should paint in the stack (say, Wings under the Back, or Face Overlay above the helm), one new EquipSlot variant, one new LAYERS position, and the paper doll grows a matching box. Existing saves are untouched - a slot nobody has worn is simply empty.
