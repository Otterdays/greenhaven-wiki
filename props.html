Signs, Chests and Campfires

Three tiles break the rule that a tile id fully describes a cell. Each carries per-cell authorship in a sidecar map on the world file, so the same tile can say something different in two places.

Signs

The wooden signpost (tile 63) is blocked scenery you can click. Its text lives in a per-cell sidecar keyed "x,y", so two signs on the same map can read differently.

  • Text. Up to 120 printable ASCII characters (newlines allowed). Anything else refuses the save - so an em dash or a curly quote cannot slip in.
  • Clickable. On by default. Off means the signpost is visible but the click does nothing.
  • Readable. On by default. Off shows the fixed line "The letters have worn away." instead of the authored text - a way to hide a lore note behind a clue, or age a sign out.

A sign is a hint, not a quest log. It never runs code, and it never moves.

Storage chests

The chest (tile 79) is blocked scenery with one usable toggle in a per-cell sidecar. A chest with usable: true opens on click; a chest with usable: false is decoration.

  • Not a shared bank. A chest is authored scenery, not a player's inventory on the wall. There is no per-player chest contents today.
  • Usable by default. If a cell has no sidecar entry, the chest is clickable. An author has to say so explicitly to turn it off.

Campfires

The campfire (tile 12) is blocked scenery with a per-cell size sidecar. Three sizes let an author pick a small cook fire, a commons hearth, or a big ceremonial blaze from the same tile.

  • Not a station. A campfire does not cook or craft today - the anvil and furnace are the stations. A campfire is light, smoke and mood.
  • Click to see, not to use. A click reports what the fire is; nothing else happens.

The sidecar pattern

Every one of these tiles is a "family tile plus per-cell metadata" pair. The tile decides the art, the collision and the click test; the sidecar decides what that one cell says, holds or does. This is what lets a map painter stamp a hundred signposts and let an author write a different line on each one without inventing a hundred tile ids.
