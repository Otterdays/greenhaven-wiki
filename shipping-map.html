Where the River Bends

The map a wanderer boots into is Where the River Bends: a 250 x 250 world the painter made, with a hamlet on the river bend, a waking trail, a mossy glade, a dock, and a scatter of hills and forest. It is the shape a fresh player learns first.

The shape of the map

The world is 250 tiles on each side. That is 62,500 cells in total - large enough to hold a hamlet, a lake, a river, three forest patches and open meadow, small enough that a fresh wanderer can cross it in a couple of minutes at a sprint. Every cell has one tile id, one height (0 to 3), and - if it carries a resource node or an authored prop - one sidecar row.

Spawn

A new character lands on the waking trail, at the map's authored spawn. The trail is a walkable path that runs from the spawn towards the hamlet. It is the first thing a wanderer sees, and it is authored to look inviting: soft grass, a scattering of flowers, no trees blocking the line of sight, a first young oak within a couple of tiles to teach the chop.

The hamlet

The river bend carries a small village: a market square, a commons with a notice board and a sundial, a few cottages with cobblestone walls and wood-plank floors, and a blacksmith's corner. Every prop is one tile from the market and home packs documented on Prop Packs. The blacksmith has the shipping map's anvil (tile 101) and a furnace (tile 113) beside it, which is where a fresh miner smelts their first bronze.

The dock and the water

A short pier of dock tiles reaches out over the river. Beside it, a stretch of boardwalk runs along the shore. The water itself is the shared water family: a wanderer cannot walk into it, but every duck, swan and fowl character floats on it and turns to face where it swims. A line of fishing spot tiles (6) sits on the shore so a minnow is one click from the dock.

The mossy glade

South of the hamlet the ground turns to moss carpet and leafy soil. A stand of pines and an ancient oak or two; a scatter of daisies, lilies, lavender and poppy; a firewood stack near a fallen stump seat; a waltz of music under the trees (the Mosslight Waltz). Every tree here is a real cuttable one, not scenery.

The hills

The north edge lifts to height 1 and 2 with a scattering of ramps (tile 24) as the only walkable ways up. Rocks cluster on the top shelf - copper, tin, then iron a little further along. A training dummy stands on the highest spot; the Wyvern and the Barrow King can be placed nearby by a builder who wants an end-game encounter on this map.

Sand and clay

The shore by the lake carries patches of sand (walkable, dig for 5 XP) and mounds of clay (blocked, dig for 8 XP). Merchant NPCs in the hamlet buy both, so a shift with a spade turns into coins.

Signs and lore

Along the waking trail and around the commons, a scatter of signposts (tile 63) hold the world's lore in short 120-character lines. A few have their readable flag turned off and say only "The letters have worn away." - a hint that the author is holding something back.

The music

Every district reads one of the six shipped songs. The hamlet loops Menu Greenhaven in the day and Lanterns at Dusk as the world's sun goes down. The river plays Where the River Bends, the map's namesake. The glade carries Mosslight Waltz, and the hills go quiet with Willowmere Lullaby. The opening score plays once, on the arrival sequence, and then hands the world over.

What is not there

As of this writing, no bosses are placed. No mobs are placed on the shipping map either - the seeded hostile set (giant rats, wild boars, wolves, bandits, wyverns, the Barrow King) waits on any map a builder adds them to. The shipping world today is a peaceful place to learn the skills and take the first bronze bar out of the furnace.
